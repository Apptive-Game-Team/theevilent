# Art Direction and Continuity

Read for pages, background revision, comic character art or setting maps.

## Separate reference roles

Inspect relevant local images and assign roles:

- Rendering: approved ink, face construction, color and shadows.
- Identity: current hair, costume, clasp, silhouette, anatomy and palette.
- Geography: relative terrain and direction, not camera, scale or labels.
- Layout: emphasis and rhythm, not events to copy.
- Continuity: a checked recent page, not automatic approval or canon.

The [comic style document](../../../../content/arcane-casters/story/COMIC-STYLE.md)
identifies the anchors. The established tutorial is useful for black oval eyes and
compact anatomy. Detailed corruption sheets can inform costume while misleading
face rendering; they do not override the comic anchor.

State reference roles and allowed cast explicitly. Obsolete creatures, dialogue,
cities and events in reference artwork must not leak into new scenes.

## Keep people and scenery in one style

Use bold variable black contours, large clear color fields, restrained hard cel
shadows and simplified silhouettes for characters and environments. Group grass
and leaves into masses; prioritize mountain outlines and broad rock planes.

Avoid realistic skin/metal, glossy CGI, delicate anime irises, soft painterly
landscapes and noisy foliage. Adult researchers do not need realistic anatomy.
Compare new faces and backgrounds with the actual anchor, not only the previous
generated page.

After establishing the setting, reduce scenery in closeups to keep hands,
expressions, cards and speech readable. Consistent style does not require equal
background density everywhere.

## A map is not a scenic camera

A map explains relative locations. A comic establishing shot conveys place, time,
mood and scale. Translate geography into a new camera; do not use a map as a
composition reference when the desired result is a lived-in landscape.

For vast terrain, use foreground, middle distance and far horizon, overlapping
hills, size relationships and cropped landmarks. Let land continue off-frame.
Showing a complete coastline and every landmark as an equally readable icon can
make a continent feel like a small island or tabletop. Overhead shots remain an
option when their role and scale suit the scene.

Consult WORLD.md before adding architecture. A terrain inspiration is not
permission to import another franchise's geography, buildings or visual style.

## Background visibility and movement

For every shot that shows the environment, decide its background from the
current map and the scene, before writing the image prompt. Briefly record:

- Where the characters and camera are, and which way the camera looks.
- Where the characters came from, their destination, and their visible movement.
- Which landmarks fall inside the view, at what distance and apparent size.
- Which landmarks must be absent because they are behind the camera, outside
  the frame, too distant, or hidden by hills, walls, roots, buildings or trees.

The mage tower is a place in the world, not a visual signature for every page.
Do not insert it into every landscape, shrink a nearby fortress into an icon,
or move it across the map to fit a composition. A distant settlement, forest or
pool may show only its local terrain. Architectural reference images constrain
the design of a visible building; they do not require that building to appear.

Check the visible direction of travel against the origin and destination.
Characters leaving a place must not appear to walk toward it. Use a clear camera
or omit the origin from the frame when showing both would confuse the route.
After a location change, preserve the route without repeating all landmarks.

Keep physical scale independent of framing: use the current size references for
people, walls, gates, golems and the World Tree. Show a huge subject through
cropped roots, walls or silhouettes when it cannot fit the shot at that scale;
do not miniaturize it for recognition. Apply the same check to final art, and
reject a page whose background or movement contradicts the map even when its
characters and rendering are otherwise usable.

## Generate and inspect

Stage props for the characters' use, not for continuous display to the viewer.
For example, documents can lie flat on a desk, slide across it, show their backs
when handed over, or be folded away. Show a content insert only when the reader
needs that information; do not repeat a front-facing diagram in every panel.

Preserve the exact prompt, target page, reference roles and printed Korean text
before generation. Specify cast, shot priorities and balloon sequence instead of
relying only on adjectives such as epic or cinematic.

Generate distinct assets individually with the available image workflow. Inspect
style, cast, reading order, world constraints and continuity before using a result
as a new reference. Correct a targeted failure and version the correction.

Save scripts, exact prompts and selected page images inside this repository.
Distinguish old attempts from selected pages. A reader or contact sheet can help
inspect the sequence but cannot substitute for requested illustrations or
authorize publication. Report actual page coverage and remaining work; repeated
or rejected variants are not additional finished pages.
