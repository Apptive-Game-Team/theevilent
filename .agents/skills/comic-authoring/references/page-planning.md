# Page and Dialogue Planning

Read for episode scripts, restructuring, panel layouts or dialogue revision.
The [craft notes](../../../../content/arcane-casters/story/references/comic-writing-notes-20261029.md)
link the original articles by Jim Zub, Steve Ellis, Teddy Wayne and CLIP STUDIO.

## Scene purpose before page count

For each scene note the starting situation, what each character wants, what
changes and what leads onward. A quiet breakfast can establish character through
small wants; it does not require a research accident or a major threat.

Use a page-by-page beat list to see where space is spent. Expand with new actions,
observations or relationship changes rather than stretching unchanged information.
Keep unresolved identities, relationships, goals and endings open.

## Compact layout template

Adapt these fields to the task; this is not a mandatory output schema:

- Page purpose, start and end states.
- Character ID, current name, immediate wish and attitude.
- Panel: selected instant, shot distance, focus and visible action.
- Exact printed speech or sound with speaker and sequence.
- Balloon space, placement and clear tail destination.
- Silent information, transition and reference roles.

Split preparation, action and reaction when progression matters. Do not ask a
single still panel to perform incompatible sequential actions, or fragment every
tiny motion when it adds no meaning or rhythm.

Establish a changed location, then choose wide views, interaction, hands, objects,
faces, silhouettes or silence as needed. Closeups need not repeat the whole
detailed landscape. Choose panel sizes for emphasis and time, not decoration.
Unequal panels and selective insets can help; diagonals everywhere do not replace
readable flow. A specific requested layout applies to that scene, not all episodes.

End a page with movement, a question, reaction, discovery or relationship change
when it helps lead onward. A cliffhanger is optional. Check reader format before
using printed-book odd/even page reveals in a single-page web reader.

## Dialogue passes

Check each exchange:

1. Why does this person speak now, and what response or action do they want?
2. Does this sound like this character in this relationship and state?
3. Can acting or an object carry some information instead?
4. Is the reply responding rather than delivering unrelated exposition?
5. Can the line be shorter while preserving tone, meaning and subtext?

Judge Korean cadence and actual balloon fit. English word-count guidelines are
not Korean limits. Do not claim an audible reading when only text was reviewed.

Preserve playful friendship without making every line a punchline or a put-down.
Show care through choices and hands. Nonverbal creatures do not get human
sentences, translated subtitles or thought monologues.

## Balloon order

Plan balloons with character placement, before finished art. For left-to-right
reading, an upper/left utterance normally precedes a lower/right reply. Avoid
crossed tails and a reply positioned to read first. Characters may change sides
with a clearly established camera.

Leave inner balloon margins and protect the important action. Check the actual
rendered text and tails, not merely what the prompt requested.
